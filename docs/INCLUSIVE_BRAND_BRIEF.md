# Inclusive Brand Brief

Status: Dimensional Futures was selected as the working brand name on October 23, 2026. Domains, trademarks, suppliers, prices, donation partner, and product claims are not yet approved.

## Portfolio structure

- **366 Industries** remains the parent organization.
- **Inclusive apparel and gifts** is the active retail project for the Shopify store.
- **Carrington Protocol** remains a separate future game and crowdfunding project. It is not ready for retail or sponsorship claims.
- **Philippines-crafted sea salt** remains a separate future food and crowdfunding project. It is not ready for retail until sourcing, compliance, packaging, shipping, and funding are validated.

The three existing Carrington and sea-salt Shopify products must stay unpublished. They may be archived only after Jordi explicitly approves that change.

## Working brand platform

**Working name:** Dimensional Futures

**Tagline:** More Ways Forward.
**Early descriptor:** Inclusive apparel & useful goods.

Supporting campaign line: More ways to see. More ways to think. More ways forward.

**Mission:** Create clothing and everyday goods that respect different ways of seeing, thinking, sensing, moving, and communicating. Products should be co-designed and physically tested with people from the communities represented, with any community contribution explained clearly and honestly.

**Positioning:** A confident, design-led lifestyle brand with an accessibility and humanitarian purpose. It must not use pity, inspiration marketing, diagnosis stereotypes, or unverified medical and accessibility claims.

**Name idea:** "Dimensional" recognizes that people experience the world through many combinations of perception, thought, communication, access, and ability. "Futures" keeps the brand optimistic and forward-looking without treating any person as something to repair.

## Visual identity direction

- **Logo concept:** a bold Dimensional Futures wordmark paired with an original open-frame "Future Fold" symbol. The nested paths suggest different viewpoints and several valid ways forward without using medical, puzzle-piece, eye, or charity imagery.
- **Core appearance:** near-black and warm off-white provide the dependable high-contrast foundation. Bright accent colors appear with distinct patterns or labels so color is never the only carrier of meaning.
- **Pattern language:** line, dot, grid, wave, and diagonal-hatch patterns can identify collections, sizes, or options independently of color.
- **Typography:** Lexend SemiBold for headlines and Atkinson Hyperlegible Next for product information and interfaces, subject to production license and file verification. Avoid highly condensed, distorted, or low-contrast text.
- **Photography:** confident people using products in real life. Show agency, personality, and collaboration rather than pity or staged inspiration.
- **Logo production rule:** maintain a strong one-color version for embroidery, tactile applications, labels, and low-cost printing.

## Brand principles

1. Design with people, not merely about them.
2. Pay community advisors and featured artists for their work.
3. Treat access as good design, not a special favor.
4. Describe exact product features instead of promising a universal experience.
5. Use color, pattern, shape, text, texture, and sound together where appropriate.
6. Publish the exact amount or percentage contributed to a named organization only after written agreements and accounting are in place.
7. Keep every product in draft until its design, supplier, sample, price, fulfillment, and claims are approved.

## Collection architecture

### Shared Futures

Universal motivational designs for the broadest first launch.

- Different Is Part of Human — graphic tee
- Make Room for Every Mind — heavyweight hoodie
- My Pace Is Still Progress — tagless tee, tote, and mug
- Access Is Everybody's Business — tote
- You Don't Have to Explain Your Pace — mug

### Many Minds

Products informed by ADHD, autistic, gifted, twice-exceptional, and other neurodivergent experiences without treating them as interchangeable.

- Focus Is Not a Moral Issue — tee
- One Thought at a Time — sweatshirt
- Quiet Is Communication — low-ink tee
- Curiosity Doesn't Clock Out — embroidered cap
- Support Is Not Special Treatment — mug
- Tools, Not Apologies — organizer pouch
- Clear Is Kind — communication patch and card set
- Low-clutter customizable planner, with no treatment claim

### Different View

Color-vision-difference products that combine color with pattern, text, and shape.

- Color Has More Than One Language — pattern-coded tee
- Same Shirt, Different View — original dual-perception graphic tee
- Pattern-Key Socks — each color represented by a distinct knit pattern and printed word
- Color, Named Clearly — mug using words, shapes, and patterns alongside color

The dual-perception design may say: "Designed to reveal differently for some people with red-green color-vision differences; what you see may vary." It must never claim that only colorblind people can see it, and it must never be marketed as a diagnostic test.

### Access Forward

This is the broader accessibility collection. Its later tactile capsule is called **Access by Touch**.

Products co-designed and tested with blind and low-vision people.

- Make Space, Not Assumptions — high-contrast tee with raised embroidery
- Ask Before Helping — tactile embroidered cap
- You Belong Here — large-print and professionally validated Braille keychain
- Know What You're Wearing — washable tactile clothing-label kit
- High-contrast mug with a removable tactile orientation and grip sleeve

Decorative or automatically generated Braille is not acceptable. Functional Braille requires a qualified transcriber, correct dot geometry, a physical production sample, durability testing, and review by Braille readers. Braille products should also offer large print, a tactile orientation marker, and an accessible web or audio description.

### Sensory Considered

Later-phase apparel with exact construction information such as fabric composition and weight, printed neck label, seam type, embroidery backing, closure force, and care instructions.

Do not call a product "sensory safe." Preferences vary. Publish the measurable features and let customers choose.

## Testing gates

Before public sale:

- Review the concept with paid people who have relevant lived experience.
- Order the exact physical sample from the intended supplier.
- Test print clarity, contrast, lighting, wash or dishwasher durability, fit, and texture as applicable.
- Give the relevant community reviewers the physical sample and document their feedback.
- Review every description for stigma, medical implications, and unsupported access claims.
- Provide accessible product pages with useful alt text, keyboard access, readable contrast, measurements, and sensory or tactile specifications.

Dual-perception color designs need printer-specific proofs and reviews from people with the color-vision types named in the copy. Screen simulations are not enough.

## Language guardrails

- Prefer specific terms such as disabled people, people with disabilities, access needs, or support needs according to community preference.
- Use ADHD rather than the outdated standalone ADD label.
- Do not use cure language, functioning labels, "suffers from," broken-versus-normal framing, gifted-superiority language, or blanket "superpower" claims.
- Do not use autism puzzle-piece imagery. Any neurodiversity symbol should be reviewed with the people represented.
- Do not conflate autism, ADHD, giftedness, blindness, intellectual disability, or other experiences.

Build only a coming-later page, development updates, email waitlist, and sponsor-interest form for now. Crowdfunding begins only after a playable prototype, tested rules, manufacturing and fulfillment quotes, campaign budget, reward tiers, schedule, and gameplay media exist.

2. Approve the first eight concepts and intended audience.
3. Select a print-on-demand or manufacturing supplier and order samples.
4. Choose whether community support is a fixed amount per item or a percentage, and select the beneficiary through a written agreement.
5. Decide whether to archive the three unrelated Shopify drafts.
